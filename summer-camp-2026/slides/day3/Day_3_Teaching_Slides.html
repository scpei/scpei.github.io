Step 2 · Search strategies
41 / 93

Greedy keeps one path; beam keeps several

Both favor high-probability sequences. The difference is how many unfinished answers survive each step.

Greedy decoding

Choose the best next token

At every step, keep only the highest-probability continuation. It is fast and consistent, but an early choice cannot be reconsidered.

1 path → 1 path → result
Beam search

Compare several partial sequences

Keep a small beam of strong continuations, expand each one, then prune back to the best few. Finally, choose the strongest completed sequence.

3 paths → expand → keep 3
[Sources] - Leblond et al., Machine Translation Decoding beyond Beam Search: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336

Step 3 · Sampling strategies
42 / 93

Temperature changes odds; top-p limits choices

They are often combined: one reshapes the probability gaps, while the other defines the candidate pool.

Temperature

Sharpen or flatten the probabilities

Low temperature makes likely tokens dominate. High temperature gives less-likely tokens a better chance before the system samples one.

low = steadier · high = more varied
Top-p sampling

Build a flexible candidate pool

Sort tokens by probability, keep the smallest group whose total reaches p, then sample from that group. The pool changes at every step.

p = 0.90 → 55% + 25% + 15%
[Sources] - Holtzman et al., The Curious Case of Neural Text Degeneration: https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09751
